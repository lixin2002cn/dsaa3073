01 / 08 · The comparison

Predict now; choose the comparator afterward

State the information order and compare with one fixed expert. PDF pp. 2–4

The main idea

Observe expert advice, commit to a decision, then reveal the outcome and all expert losses. Use that feedback on later rounds. The target is the best single expert on the same sequence.

\[R_T=\sum_{t=1}^T L_t(\mathcal A)-\min_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_t(i).\]

The minimum follows the sum: one expert is used across the entire horizon.

Past + expert adviceCommitReveal all lossesUpdate for tomorrow
Define the objects and the feedback

There are \(n\ge1\) experts and \(T\ge1\) rounds. An expert is an advice rule, with no promise of accuracy. The vector \(\ell_t=(\ell_t(1),\ldots,\ell_t(n))\) contains all expert losses on round \(t\); the learner incurs the scalar \(L_t(\mathcal A)\). With binary zero-one loss, 0 means correct and 1 means wrong.

If losses are \((0,1,1)\) and the learner follows expert 1, its loss is 0, not the sum 2. Full information reveals every coordinate after commitment. Revealing only the selected coordinate would define a different feedback problem.

The sequence need not be independent or sampled from one distribution. This is a comparison of realized sequential losses, rather than a population-risk statement.

Compute the source example and interpret average regret

The source gives learner losses \((1,0,1)\), expert 1 losses \((0,1,1)\), and expert 2 losses \((1,0,0)\). Their totals are \(2,2,1\); hence regret is \(2-1=1\). Choosing a zero-loss expert separately on every round gives 0, but changes the comparator.

\[\frac1T\sum_tL_t(\mathcal A)=\frac1T\min_i\sum_t\ell_t(i)+\frac{R_T}{T}.\]

If every expert loses one every round, following experts has average loss 1 and regret 0. Conversely, a learner that changes decisions can beat every fixed expert, giving negative regret.

The definition \(R_T=o(T)\) says the actual ratio tends to zero. A sublinear upper guarantee \(R_T\le b(T)\), \(b(T)/T\to0\), controls only the upper excess average loss: actual regret can stay substantially negative.

Practice · added checks

1. Locate the minimum

The expert loss vectors are \((0,1)\) then \((1,0)\). Find the best fixed total and the switching-oracle total.

Show solution

Each fixed expert totals 1, so the comparator is 1. A round-by-round oracle obtains 0. External regret uses the former.

2. Separate feedback and performance

May full information reveal \(\ell_t\) before the current decision? Does zero regret imply zero average loss?

Show solution

No to both. All coordinates arrive after commitment. When every expert loses one every round, regret is zero but average learner loss is one.

One comparator

Sum each expert's whole trajectory, then minimize. Scalar learner loss and the expert loss vector are different objects.

02 / 08 · A realizable start

A surviving expert turns shrinkage into a bound

Identify the exact role of a perfect expert. PDF pp. 5–7

The main idea

Halving retains experts that have never been wrong. Predict their majority label, with tie label 0, then remove wrong experts after every outcome. Every learner mistake removes at least half the active set. A perfect expert supplies a survivor.

\[1\le |S_{T+1}|\le n/2^M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M\le\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor.\]
Follow the same four experts

Initialize \(S_1=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). After outcome \(y_t\), retain exactly the active experts whose advice equals \(y_t\). Removal also occurs when the learner is correct.

Round 1 ties: prediction 0 is wrong, and experts 1 and 2 leave. Round 2 also ties among active experts: prediction 0 is correct, but expert 4 is wrong and leaves. Expert 3 remains. A third outcome of 1 keeps it perfect; an outcome of 0 empties the set.

Prove both sides of the survivor argument

On a learner mistake, at least half the active experts voted for the learner's wrong label, including a tied vote. Thus \(|S_{t+1}|\le |S_t|/2\). The set never grows on other rounds, yielding \(|S_{T+1}|\le n/2^M\).

A perfect expert is never removed, so \(|S_{T+1}|\ge1\). Combine these inequalities and take a base-two logarithm. Since \(M\) is integral, the floor applies. The bound counts mistakes, not elapsed rounds; there may be many correct rounds and multiple surviving experts.

On the no-perfect-expert branch, shrinkage remains valid but the nonempty-set lower bound is unavailable. A best expert in hindsight may make mistakes, so that weaker premise does not establish this theorem.

On source round 2, the learner is right. Which expert leaves and why?

Show solution

Expert 4 leaves because it predicts 1 while the outcome is 0. Removal records each expert's own correctness.

2. Use the premise

For eight experts with one perfect expert, bound learner mistakes. Would a promise of an expert with two mistakes suffice?

Show solution

The premise has a job

A perfect expert supplies the lower bound 1. “Best in hindsight” does not itself supply a survivor.

03 / 08 · Retain imperfect advice

Reduce influence without deleting an expert

Reproduce the updates and their factor-style guarantee. PDF pp. 7–10

The main idea

Initialize \(w_1(i)=1\), fix \(0\lt\eta\le1/2\), and predict the greater-weight label, using 0 for ties. After feedback, multiply wrong experts by \(1-\eta\) and keep correct experts' weights unchanged.

\[w_{t+1}(i)=w_t(i)(1-\eta)^{\ell_t(i)},\qquad \ell_t(i)\in\{0,1\}.\]

All weights stay positive. A correct expert can gain relative influence without gaining absolute weight.

Reproduce the source's three-round calculation

Use the advice table with outcomes \((1,0,0)\) and \(\eta=1/2\). The pre-decision weights are

\[(1,1,1,1),\quad(1/2,1/2,1,1),\quad(1/2,1/4,1,1/2).\]

The learner predicts \(0,0,1\), making two mistakes. Expert totals are \((2,2,1,1)\), so regret is 1. Final weights are \((1/4,1/4,1/2,1/2)\). Experts 2 and 4 are reduced even on the learner's correct second round.

If expert \(i\) makes \(M_i\) mistakes, \(w_{T+1}(i)=(1-\eta)^{M_i}\). Define \(\Phi_t=\sum_iw_t(i)\), with \(\Phi_1=n\). The index \(T+1\) means after all updates. Total weight is a proof quantity, not a probability or learner loss.

Derive the learner and comparator bounds

On a learner mistake at least half the weight is wrong, so \(\Phi_{t+1}\le(1-\eta/2)\Phi_t\). On other rounds total weight cannot increase. Using \(1-z\le e^{-z}\),

\[\Phi_{T+1}\le n(1-\eta/2)^M\le n e^{-\eta M/2}.\]

Every positive expert weight is included in total weight. The source also proves \(-\log(1-x)\le x+x^2\) on \([0,1/2]\): the function \(x+x^2+\log(1-x)\) starts at zero and has derivative \(x(1-2x)/(1-x)\ge0\). Therefore

\[e^{-\eta(1+\eta)M_i}\le(1-\eta)^{M_i}\le\Phi_{T+1}\le n e^{-\eta M/2}.\]

Take natural logarithms, rearrange, and divide by positive \(\eta/2\):

\[M\le 2(1+\eta)M_i+\frac{2\log n}{\eta}.\]

The result holds for every fixed expert, including the best one. If \(M^*\) grows with \(T\), this factor-style comparison does not force additive regret \(M-M^*\) to be sublinear. Logarithms without a base are natural.

Explain the deterministic binary lower bound

Two experts always predict 0 and 1. An opponent that knows a deterministic binary decision can select the opposite outcome, giving the learner \(M=T\). Exactly one expert is wrong per round, so \(M_1+M_2=T\) and \(M^*\le T/2\). Hence \(R_T\ge T/2\).

For weighted majority with equal weights and tie 0, outcomes alternate \(1,0,1,0,\ldots\). With even \(T\), each expert makes \(T/2\) errors. A smaller learning rate changes weight values but does not hide the deterministic decision. The claim concerns binary deterministic decisions, not every real-valued prediction method.

Update every expert

Each expert's own revealed loss controls its reduction. A correct learner round may still reduce total weight.

04 / 08 · Change the decision

Keep the current draw private

Distinguish a probability vector, a sampled index, and an expectation. PDF pp. 11–12, 14

The main idea

Normalize \(p_t(i)=w_t(i)/\Phi_t\), privately draw \(I_t\) from this distribution, and follow that expert. Under nonanticipating timing, the current loss vector is fixed without seeing the current draw.

\[\mathbb E[\ell_t(I_t)\mid p_t,\ell_t,\text{pre-draw information}]=\sum_i p_t(i)\ell_t(i)=\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle.\]

A binary draw loses 0 or 1. A mixture loss of \(1/2\) describes its conditional expectation.

Publish probabilitiesFix hidden loss vectorDraw privatelyReveal + update
What may the opponent know?

A nonanticipating opponent may use the past and the current public distribution, but not the current private draw when fixing current losses. Later vectors may depend on earlier sampled actions. An oblivious opponent fixes the whole sequence in advance and satisfies this restriction.

Fixing a hidden vector differs from revealing it. The learner cannot inspect that vector before deciding. If the opponent instead sees the current selected action before assigning current losses, the stated guarantee does not apply.

Since all losses are revealed, updates do not depend on the sampled index. For a fixed sequence, deterministic and randomized weighted majority have the same weights but different decision rules and possibly different learner losses.

Normalize the source weights
Round\(p_t\)\(\ell_t\)Mixture loss
1\((1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)\)\((1,1,0,0)\)\(1/2\)
2\((1/6,1/6,1/3,1/3)\)\((0,1,0,1)\)\(1/2\)
3\((2/9,1/9,4/9,2/9)\)\((1,0,1,0)\)\(2/3\)

The cumulative mixture loss is \(5/3\). The best fixed expert totals 1, so the mixture gap is \(2/3\). Deterministic voting loses 2 and has regret 1 on this same sequence. A sampled run need not realize the fractional expected total.

The expectation sums each possible coordinate loss times its selection probability. It holds conditionally on the already fixed vector and does not require independence between rounds.

Condition on the vector

A public distribution is distinct from its current realized index. Fix the current vector without observing this draw.

05 / 08 · General bounded losses

Keep the update linear in the current loss

Extend binary feedback to \([0,1]\) while preserving the information order. PDF pp. 13–15

The main idea

Loss 0.3 now means 0.3 units of cost, without necessarily being a mistake probability. With equal initial weights, fixed \(0\lt\eta\le1/2\), and the full vector \(\ell_t\in[0,1]^n\), update

\[w_{t+1}(i)=w_t(i)(1-\eta\ell_t(i)).\]

The multiplier is linear in the current loss. It lies in \([1-\eta,1]\), so finite-round weights remain positive and normalization remains defined.

Continue the source sequence with a fractional fourth round

After the three binary rounds, the incoming weights are \((1/4,1/4,1/2,1/2)\). At \(\eta=1/2\), round 4 explicitly changes the feedback to \(\ell_4=(0,1/2,1,1/4)\). Pre-round probabilities are \((1/6,1/6,1/3,1/3)\). Therefore

\[\langle p_4,\ell_4\rangle=\tfrac16\cdot0+\tfrac16\cdot\tfrac12+\tfrac13\cdot1+\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac14=\tfrac12.\]

The factors are \((1,3/4,1/2,7/8)\). Next weights are \((1/4,3/16,1/4,7/16)\), total \(9/8\), and probabilities \((2/9,1/6,2/9,7/18)\). A sampled expert realizes one coordinate loss, while the mixture averages those coordinates.

This weights experts, whereas AdaBoost weighted training observations with an exponential factor. The broad analogy does not replace an analysis of this particular update. An unbounded loss could make the linear factor negative, outside the assumptions.

Why are all legal multipliers at least \(1/2\)? In the source fourth round, verify the new total from \(\Phi_4=3/2\) and mixture loss \(1/2\).

Same experts, new loss domain

The fourth round is fractional feedback, not a hidden binary outcome. The same full-information update still uses every expert's loss.

06 / 08 · The regret proof

Connect learner loss to a fixed expert through total weight

Prove the pathwise mixture inequality, then interpret sampling. PDF pp. 14–18

The main idea

For mixture loss \(m_t=\langle p_t,\ell_t\rangle\), the exact total-weight update is

\[\Phi_{t+1}=\sum_iw_t(i)(1-\eta\ell_t(i))=\Phi_t(1-\eta m_t).\]

This identity records the exact mixture loss on every round. One expert's retained weight lower-bounds the same total that learner loss upper-bounds.

\[\sum_{t=1}^T m_t-L^*\le \frac{\log n}{\eta}+\eta L^*\le\frac{\log n}{\eta}+\eta T,\qquad L^*=\min_i\sum_t\ell_t(i).\]
1. Upper-bound total weight using mixture loss

Because \(m_t\in[0,1]\), the exact factor is positive. Apply \(1-z\le e^{-z}\) on each round, then multiply from the initial total \(n\):

\[\Phi_{T+1}=n\prod_{t=1}^T(1-\eta m_t)\le n\exp\!\left(-\eta\sum_{t=1}^T m_t\right).\]

No expectation has been taken. The statement is deterministic for the distributions and losses produced on a realized sequence. Unlike the deterministic-vote proof's half-weight estimate on mistakes, it tracks the exact mixture mass every round.

2. Lower-bound total weight using one expert

Fix an expert \(i\), write \(L_i=\sum_t\ell_t(i)\), and expand its weight product. For \(x\in[0,1]\), convexity of \(x\mapsto(1-\eta)^x\) puts the curve below its endpoint chord:

\[(1-\eta)^x\le1-\eta x.\]

Its second derivative is \((\log(1-\eta))^2(1-\eta)^x\ge0\). Multiplying positive factors gives

\[\Phi_{T+1}\ge w_{T+1}(i)=\prod_t(1-\eta\ell_t(i))\ge(1-\eta)^{L_i}\ge e^{-\eta(1+\eta)L_i}.\]

The last step uses the earlier logarithm inequality. The exponential is a lower bound in the proof; the algorithm itself still uses linear multipliers. Binary losses give equality in the chord step.

3. Combine, rearrange, then subtract the comparator
\[e^{-\eta(1+\eta)L_i}\le\Phi_{T+1}\le n e^{-\eta\sum_tm_t}.\]

Take logarithms, move the learner term to the left, and divide by positive \(\eta\). This gives a cumulative loss comparison for every expert:

\[\sum_tm_t\le(1+\eta)L_i+\frac{\log n}{\eta}.\]

Choose a best fixed expert and subtract \(L^*\). The sharper expression is \(\eta L^*+\log(n)/\eta\). Since each coordinate loss is at most one, \(L^*\le T\), giving the coarse expression that can be tuned without knowing the best expert beforehand.

For the source's four rounds, expert totals are \((2,5/2,2,5/4)\) and mixture loss is \(13/6\). The gap is \(13/6-5/4=11/12\). The theorem upper-bounds this gap; it does not predict equality with its bound.

4. Transfer the pathwise result to expected sampled regret

For private sampling, realized learner loss is \(\ell_t(I_t)\). Nonanticipation and total expectation give

\[\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_t\ell_t(I_t)\right]=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_tm_t\right].\]

If later losses adapt to past draws, the sequence and its best-expert total can both be random. Subtract the same random comparator inside both expectations:

\[\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_t\ell_t(I_t)-\min_i\sum_t\ell_t(i)\right]=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_tm_t-\min_i\sum_t\ell_t(i)\right]\le\frac{\log n}{\eta}+\eta T.\]

Keep the minimum inside the expectation. Do not replace an expected minimum by a minimum of expectations. An oblivious fixed sequence has a deterministic comparator and permits it outside. This is an expected-regret guarantee, not a claim about every sampled run or a high-probability statement.

Practice · added checks

1. Use the shared middle quantity

Why does the upper bound alone fail to prove small regret? Where does the comparator enter?

Show solution

The upper bound involves learner loss but no expert loss. The comparator enters through \(w_{T+1}(i)\le\Phi_{T+1}\) and the individual-weight product. Both bounds constrain one total.

2. Locate the expectation

For an adaptive nonanticipating opponent, may we move \(\min_i\sum_t\ell_t(i)\) outside the expectation as a fixed constant?

Show solution

No. Future losses can depend on past sampled actions, so the best fixed expert's total may be random. Keep that same random comparator inside the expectation.

Two levels of guarantee

The mixture inequality holds on every realized sequence. The sampled version takes expectations using nonanticipating timing.

07 / 08 · Tune within the theorem

Balance the bound without leaving the legal interval

Separate bound contributions from measured losses. PDF pp. 18–20

The main idea

For \(n\ge2\) and known horizon \(T\), balance \(B(\eta)=\log(n)/\eta+\eta T\), subject to the required \(0\lt\eta\le1/2\):

\[\eta_{\mathrm{legal}}=\min\!\left\{\frac12,\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{T}}\right\}.\]

When \(T\ge4\log n\), this gives \(R_T\le2\sqrt{T\log n}\) for mixture regret and the same bound for expected sampled regret.

Differentiate, cap, and handle the edge cases

The derivative is \(B'(\eta)=-\log(n)/\eta^2+T\). Its zero gives the square-root candidate. If it exceeds \(1/2\), use the endpoint \(1/2\). In the capped regime \(T\lt4\log n\), the coarse bound is \(2\log n+T/2\). Independently, regret is at most \(T\), since learner loss is at most \(T\) and best expert loss is nonnegative.

\(n,T\)Unconstrained rateLegal rateAvailable bound
\(4,64\)\(0.1472\)\(0.1472\)\(2\sqrt{64\log4}\approx18.84\)
\(4,1\)\(1.1774\)\(1/2\)Coarse \(3.27\); trivial bound 1 is sharper

If \(n=1\), learner and comparator coincide and regret is exactly zero. Keep a legal positive rate, such as \(1/2\); substituting \(\sqrt{\log1/T}=0\) into a formula dividing by \(\eta\) is invalid.

Interpret the horizon and the average guarantee

For fixed \(n\) and sufficiently large known horizons, the balanced upper excess per round is \(2\sqrt{\log(n)/T}\), tending to zero. This is a family of runs, each with its own rate fixed before play.

A single positive fixed rate leaves a coarse average term \(\eta\). Changing \(\eta\) every round is a different schedule and requires a different summation argument. The two curves in the lab are upper-bound contributions, rather than measured components of regret.

08 / 08 · Synthesis

Let the assumptions choose the rule

Connect the algorithms, their comparisons, and the vocabulary. PDF pp. 20–21

The main idea

Positive weights retain imperfect experts. Private sampling changes the decision rule when its timing condition holds. A two-sided potential argument connects cumulative mixture loss to any fixed expert, allowing the best one to be chosen afterward.

A perfect-expert task can use survivor counting. Removing that promise motivates positive weights. Requiring deterministic binary decisions permits the source's factor-style theorem but also its linear-regret construction. Allowing private draws under the correct information order gives the sampled interpretation. Fractional feedback calls for the general linear update.
